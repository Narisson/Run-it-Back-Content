# The Six C's — winning on social, distilled

Source: founder-curated research (Forbes top-10 creator's "if I started over in 2026"
framework; watched 2026-07-29, video id `ZvAwqGH_79Q`). Their numbers/examples are from a
business/finance audience — treat benchmarks as directional for our niche. Adapted to
Run it Back; companion to `channel-launch-playbook.md` (that one = channel strategy + video
craft; this one = platform selection, credibility, idea supply, metrics, and money).

Read this when: choosing where to focus effort across platforms, doing the weekly metrics
review, refilling the idea backlog, questioning production quality level, or designing the
monetization ladder.

---

## C1. Concentration — win ONE platform, then expand

Spray-and-pray (many average posts across every platform) is how most people fail. You win
by being **undeniable in one place** first. Each platform is a different audience with a
different language:

| Platform | Personality | What wins | What flops | Sales power |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **TikTok** | 14-y/o cousin, zero patience (2s) | stripped-down zero-production "yapping," contrarian hook <4s, problem→solution | slow builds, polish | Awareness machine, **terrible at off-platform sales** (reaches ~38% cold audience vs followers; they sold <12 event tickets off 2M followers). Books/visual products via Shop are the exception |
| **Instagram** | aspirational friend, peek-behind-curtain | **shareable** moments (a DM share ≈ 3–5× a like for reach), casual/relatable > lecture, carousels (rising) + reels (growth) | value-dump lectures, projecting at the audience | Engaged + valuable; sold ~100× TikTok's tickets |
| **YouTube** | wants the full tactical playbook | story-driven education, case studies, named frameworks early; content lives longest, trust builds fastest; community posts = underused growth/sales lever | vague self-improvement ("outwork everyone") with no immediate problem solved | Best monetization (sponsorship + sales); algorithm currently favors small channels + less-produced content |
| **X** | cerebral, wants a fight | contrarian takes, one-liners, predictions that invite argument; horizontal podcast clips; content dies in ~a day | feel-good aesthetic content trying to be liked | Lowest value per follower |
| **LinkedIn** | professionals getting smarter/richer | tactical education + professional aspiration, quote-image + value caption, lead magnets | press-release energy, corporate fluff | Sleeper for B2B/hiring niches (not ours, low priority) |
| **Newsletter** | highest-intent audience, fully owned | frameworks, "here's what I'd do," breakdowns | filler (trains people to stop opening) | **Sells the most of any channel** (30% of their tickets) — validates our email-from-day-one rule |

**RIB application:** keep cheap cross-posting (re-export is nearly free), but concentrate
*iteration effort* on one platform until it's won — this sharpens the existing rule "if a
platform gives 3× traction, shift 70% of effort there" into a default posture, not just a
trigger. Growth tip: follow the platform's own active employees/execs — they post free
signal about what the platform wants next.

## C2. Credibility — costs nothing, and production can HURT it

Their data: the more produced their content, the worse it performed (kitchen-phone video
≈ 100× the views of the polished studio version). The fake-podcast-studio look now reads
as inauthentic. Three free credibility sources:

1. **Platform-native:** look like you belong — each platform has its own font/pacing/edit
   feel. Consume the platform enough to speak its local language; native-feel earns trust
   before a word is said.
2. **History:** don't ask "what should I post" — ask **"what have I earned the right to
   explain?"** The dinner test: could you talk 30 minutes on it, zero prep, while friends
   grill you? (RIB: the founder has earned — living the fall-off/restart loop, the
   designer/maker craft, honest gear ownership. Not earned: guru-level claims about
   outcomes we haven't had.)
3. **In-action:** show yourself *doing the thing*, where it happens (their example: a
   construction crew filming on real job sites — nothing to fake). RIB: film the actual
   reps, the actual desk, the actual missed-day-and-restart. Build-in-public is this.

This independently confirms our imperfect-on-purpose launch stance and the low-fi winning
formats in `knowledge-base/viral-patterns.md`.

## C3. Category — own one thing, repeat past boredom

Winners are known for ONE thing said over and over (the debt guy, the productivity guy).
Repetition feels boring to the creator long before the audience — a normal post reaches
only a small slice of followers, so nobody else is bored.

- **Own a word/phrase:** use the exact phrase relentlessly in content. RIB already has two:
  **"run it back"** and **"never miss twice."** Say them constantly; they are the category claim.
- **Own a visual:** a repeated visual signature makes you recognizable without a word
  (RIB: the ↻ mark, the visual system, the sign-off).
- **Pillars = repeatable formats** run again and again — this is the series/franchise
  structure we already have; the research confirms it's the mechanism, not a preference.
- Niche economics note (YouTube RPM): finance $5–17 / education-career $4–10 /
  entertainment $4–6 per 1k views. Self-improvement-with-mechanism sits nearer education —
  another reason Discipline Lab (teach the science) beats pure motivation content.

## C4. The idea supply system — never pull content from thin air

The audience is already saying what it wants. Four steps, kept running:

1. **Swipe file:** collect top-performing posts in the niche (target: ~100 over time) —
   screenshot the packaging, transcribe the first 30 seconds, log the top comments and who
   sponsors them (the sponsor list doubles as a future revenue map). Our vidiQ outlier
   sweeps (`knowledge-base/viral-patterns.md`) are this file — keep it fed.
2. **Steal the hook, not the content:** proven hook/topic + only-we-can-say-it substance
   (our mechanism, our comeback POV, our maker's honesty). Write scripts in bulk (they
   write 30 before filming) — scripting is cheap; filming unvalidated ideas isn't.
3. **Mine the comments:** the lazy copy the top video; the smart read the **unanswered
   questions underneath it** (and in subreddits/Discords). A viral video whose comments
   scream an unmet question = the next video, pre-validated.
4. **Problem bank in THEIR words:** log audience phrasing verbatim ("I don't have the
   money and I'm scared I'll look stupid," not "capital constraints"). Speak it back and
   they think you read their mind. RIB: collect the exact words people use about falling
   off — from our own comments/DMs from day one.

## C5. Calibration — data is a map, not a judgment

**The three S's per post:** did they **Start** (hook strength) → **Stay** (payoff speed)
→ **Signal** (do anything after)? No stops = weak hook; start-then-bail = payoff too slow;
watched-but-no-action = entertaining, not memorable.

Per-platform scoreboard (their benchmarks; directional):

| Platform | Primary metric | Good |
|---|---|---|
| YouTube | CTR + watch time | CTR 4–7%; flat retention line |
| Instagram | share rate + watch-through | shares ~4%; watch-through >50% (>60% on 15s clips). Tip: hide like counts so slow posts aren't punished by low social proof |
| TikTok | completion + comments | (our existing >70% completion target stands) |
| X | reply rate | 1–3% = excellent; takes that make people *talk* |
| Newsletter | open rate | 30% baseline; 40%+ = crushing |

**Reading retention:** name the framework/value early (22 seconds in = an eternity);
never end on a summary (it's always the drop-off); the goal is a flat line after the hook.

**The 3-note post-mortem (every post):** ① why did they start watching? ② where did they
leave? ③ which line got a reaction? Do this across ~50 posts and patterns become
undeniable. **If you can't name why a post worked, you can't repeat it — luck is a stupid
strategy.** This slots directly into the weekly 30-min review.

## C6. Collect — organic sells; own the ladder

Platform ad revenue is the smallest slice. Their campaign data: 62% of sales came from
organic (vs 38% paid) — organic *owns* the relationship, ads rent it. The structure is a
**product ladder** solving the same person's problem at increasing depth:

| Rung | Price | Form | RIB (per MASTER-PLAN) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | free | content + newsletter capture | The Reminder files, email list (Phase 2) |
| 2 | $0–50 | easy-yes guide/template/book | 30-day comeback system (Phase 4) |
| 3 | $50–500 | playbook/community/course | the Comeback Club (Phase 5) |
| 4 | $500–5k | access/cohort | later, if earned |
| 5 | $5k+ | hyper-personalized | not our game |

**Build one rung at a time; only add the next when the one below is full.** This is
exactly MASTER-PLAN's gated phases — independent confirmation of the structure. Newsletter
= the highest-converting channel, which is why email capture starts at day one even though
selling waits.

**Ship at 85%:** the last 15% of polish takes weeks and no one notices it. (Their words;
also our Phase 0 in one sentence.)
